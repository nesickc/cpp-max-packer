# T-018: atomic checkpoints and interrupted-process recovery

Status: planned; no implementation or passing evidence claimed.
Proposed branch: `feature/DATA-02-checkpoint-recovery` (not created).
Prerequisites: [T-012](T-012.md) and [T-017](T-017.md), merged into `main`.
Start the branch only after those prerequisites and this planning change merge.
Shared delivery/evidence rules: [MILESTONES.md](MILESTONES.md) and
[ADR 0011](../spec/decisions/0011-cpu-first-follow-up.md).

## Scope and boundary

Persist immutable validated revisions and atomically replace the checkpoint
pointer/project archive. Recover the last complete result after process failure,
then Continue with compatible state or an explicitly reset warm start. Retain
lossless source/accepted repair evidence. Search/device caches are disposable;
GPU buffers cannot be the sole recoverable state. Device-loss Auto policy is
T-020; live viewer revisions are T-021, not prerequisites for durable recovery.

## Governing requirements

SOL-06/SOL-07, DATA-01/DATA-02/DATA-03 and UI-03; AT-13/AT-14 and CPU
AT-16 subsets. Canonical spec §§8.2–8.5 and 10.1 govern; M3 recovery remains
open until process-level tests pass, not merely portable Save/Open tests.

## Observable acceptance

1. **T018-A1:** Checkpoint every new best and at least every 30 s at a safe
   boundary. Store a complete validated revision, immutable settings/assets and
   supported RNG/search state; record boundary delays instead of hiding overruns.
2. **T018-A2:** Kill during a mutable trial and at each checkpoint/archive
   replacement boundary. Reopen the last complete valid version; partial files
   cannot replace it. Verify replacement ordering and retain the previous version
   until a temporary write and atomic rename have succeeded.
3. **T018-A3:** Restore hashes, schemas and authoritative placements before
   activating an incumbent. Move archives between directories/user profiles;
   preserve Unicode/spaced/long paths, source frames and accepted repaired assets.
4. **T018-A4:** Compatible engine/search/cache versions resume recorded state;
   incompatible but valid poses warm-start with an explicit reset. Preserve
   segment lineage, budget/RNG/thread/backend changes and monotone best count;
   never claim bit-for-bit continuation after an incompatible backend change.
5. **T018-A5:** Corrupt/truncate checkpoints, repair evidence or archives; tamper
   hashes/matrices and attempt traversal/excessive sizes. Reject safely without
   damaging unrelated files or the retained complete checkpoint/project.
6. **T018-A6:** Inject full disk, failed writes/rename and host allocation failure.
   Keep prior valid data, specific errors and usable retry/export. An interrupted
   STL export must not invalidate the primary JSON/project or its valid poses.
7. **T018-A7:** Stop acknowledges within 250 ms; ordinary safe completion is
   within 5 s on qualified cases. For an unresponsive native operation, offer
   termination after 10 s while keeping the UI interactive and the last checkpoint
   recoverable. Revalidate after termination before resumed search or export.

## Tests, evidence and review

Use practical red/green process-kill and real file-system failure cases, including
trial/replacement timing barriers. Independently inspect restored counts/poses,
checksums, repair provenance and export coordinates. Retain full logs, actual
commands and compatible/reset traces under `.local/t018/`; shared evidence applies.
Architect review settles persistence ownership/schema compatibility. Atomicity,
recovery and cancellation concurrency require focused critical review.

## Open decisions and compatibility

Freeze durable pointer/write ordering, checkpoint versioning, retention policy,
search-state keys and legacy portable-project migration before edits. Define
supported Windows durability guarantees explicitly; atomic rename alone must
not imply untested power-loss guarantees. No new archive/dependency is selected.
